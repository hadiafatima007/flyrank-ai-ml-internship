Content Decay & Refresh Prioritization

A ranked, reason-coded action model for deciding which pages in a search content portfolio deserve refresh attention first — and whether a learned model beats a transparent hand-written rule at that job.

Author Hadia Fatima Repo github.com/hadiafatima007/flyrank-ai-ml-internship
STALE_REFRESH → Refresh Content CTR_FIX → Improve Title & Meta QUICK_WIN → Optimise Existing Content LOW_PRIORITY → Monitor
Abstract

Which pages in a content portfolio should a human reviewer prioritize for refresh, and does a learned model rank them better than a simple, auditable rule? A transparent baseline rule (staleness, search demand, near-page-one ranking, low CTR) and a client-grouped Logistic Regression classifier were each trained and evaluated on 30,000 anonymized content records spanning 32 clients, using an 80/20 split by client_id so no client appeared in both sets. On the held-out clients, the model reached a Precision@50 of 0.72 against 0.54 for the rule-based baseline — an observed 18-percentage-point improvement on this split. Recent engagement recency and consistency (90-day sessions and users, days with impressions or sessions) carried the strongest associations with the decline label, more than raw content age or search volume alone. The output is a ranked, reason-coded queue for human review, not a certified prediction of business impact.

03Methodology

Baseline: a transparent scoring rule

The Week-4 baseline gives points for four signals: staleness (days_since_last_update > 180 → +40), search demand (search_volume >= 100 → +30), ranking opportunity (avg_position between 8 and 20 → +20), and a CTR opportunity (ctr < 2 → +10). The resulting score creates a ranked review queue and outputs one of four reason codes:

STALE_REFRESH
Old page, refresh opportunity → Refresh Content
CTR_FIX
CTR below expectation for its rank → Improve Title & Meta
QUICK_WIN
Near page one, good demand → Optimise Existing Content
LOW_PRIORITY
Doesn't meet key signals → Monitor

Model: Logistic Regression

Logistic Regression was chosen because the task is ranking, not just classifying: it produces a probability that can order a priority queue, it's simple enough to inspect coefficient-by-coefficient, and it gives a fair, interpretable first comparison against a hand-written rule before reaching for anything more complex.

Label: 1 when trend_direction == "down", else 0 — an observed historical outcome, not a future prediction. Numeric features were median-imputed and standardized; categorical features were mode-imputed and one-hot encoded (max_iter=1000). Numeric inputs: search_volume, competition, cpc, word_count, char_count, impressions_90d, clicks_90d, pageviews_90d, sessions_90d, users_90d, engaged_sessions_90d, ai_sessions_90d, scroll_events_90d, days_with_impressions, days_with_sessions, content_age_days, age_tier_order, days_since_last_update, ctr, avg_position, engagement_rate, scroll_rate, ai_traffic_pct. Categorical inputs: competition_level, content_type, main_intent, provider_used.

Split design

Rows were split by grouping on client_id rather than at random, because pages belonging to the same client tend to share characteristics — a random row split would let the model partly memorize client identity instead of learning transferable signal.

Leakage audit

Fields describing the outcome itself, or derived from the baseline's own output, were excluded from the model's features:

  • trend_direction, trend_pct — these define the label
  • impressions_last_30d, impressions_prev_30d — these directly feed the 30-day trend comparison the label is built from
  • the baseline's own score, reason code, and action outputs — decision outputs, not predictive inputs

Remaining historical traffic and content attributes were treated as inputs describing observed state, not future-window or label-derived information.

Grounding in prior research

Two findings from the audited literature shaped which signals were trusted at face value rather than taken as causal rules. First, content performance was reported to peak around 61–90 days and decline afterward, with 365+ day recovery concentrated specifically in refreshed older pages — the source paper itself cautions against reading that as age improving performance on its own. Second, high search volume was reported as a weak predictor of page health by itself, with some lower-volume pages performing better — treated here as context rather than a standalone signal. Both findings are observational associations; neither was used to assert that any single feature causes decline or recovery.

05Limitations & honest framing

This is decision-support evidence, not proof. The 18-point precision gain is an observed result on one held-out client split with one random seed — it describes what happened on this evaluation, not a guaranteed future gain, and it does not establish that any feature causes decline.
  • Correlational, not causal. Coefficients show association with the decline label, not a mechanism.
  • Single split, single seed. No cross-validation across multiple client folds was reported — the reported gap could shift with a different held-out client group.
  • Label is directional by construction. "Declining" comes from an observed trend field, not an independent ground truth of business harm.
  • Portfolio-specific. 30,000 pages across 32 clients may not represent a different content mix, vertical, or traffic profile.
  • The score is a priority signal, not a probability of success. A high rank means "review this," not "refreshing this will work."

06Ranked recommendations

The action playbook exports every page as a ranked, reason-coded row for human review — ordered by score, highest priority first. It is a queue for a person to work through, not an autonomous action engine.

RankReason codeRecommended actionSample score
1STALE_REFRESHRefresh Content100.0
2–4STALE_REFRESHRefresh Content80.0
5–20STALE_REFRESHRefresh Content70.0

Top of the 30,000-row queue is dominated by stale, low-engagement pages sitting in strong ranking positions — the highest-leverage refresh candidates, per the baseline rule's own logic. The full ranked queue is exported to outputs/action_playbook_queue.csv.

Archetype → action mapping

  • Stale content → STALE_REFRESH → Refresh Content
  • Existing content with a quick opportunity → QUICK_WIN → Optimise Existing Content
  • Ambiguous or weak signals → human review before assigning an action
